# Exploratory Machine Learning and Multivariate Analysis of Shoot Metabolic Responses to Chromium Stress in Amaranthus tricolor and Spinacia oleracea

Chromium (Cr), particularly in its hexavalent form, is toxic to plants and may accumulate in edible tissues. We investigated shoot responses of the C4 leafy vegetable Amaranthus tri-color and the C3 leafy vegetable Spinacia oleracea grown in clay soil with or without Cr(VI) (350 mg kg⁻¹ soil) for three months. Shoot Cr increased from about 0.5 to 53 (A. tricolor) and 65 µg g⁻¹ DW (spinach), and root Cr to about 91–94 µg g⁻¹ DW. Cr reduced net photosyn-thesis by 42%, Rubisco activity by 38–42% and fresh weight by 40–43% in both species. Of 24 shoot metabolites (sugars, organic acids, fatty acids), 15 differed between treatments after false-discovery-rate correction, mostly increased fatty acids and organic acids. Re-sponses were stronger in spinach, where 12 metabolites changed significantly within the species, than in A. tricolor, where none did after correction, and nine metabolites showed a significant species × treatment interaction. Random Forest classified control and Cr-treated plants with 88% accuracy under nested leave-one-out validation (permutation p = 0.02) and ranked heptadecanoic, palmitoleic, palmitic, linoleic and oleic acids highest, but PLS-DA and logistic regression performed equally well or better. Support vector re-gression predicted Rubisco activity from metabolites (leave-one-out Q² = 0.83) but not bet-ter than a model with treatment and species alone, and predicted other traits poorly. We therefore interpret the metabolite rankings as hypothesis-generating candidate indicators of Cr(VI) stress that need validation in larger, independent experiments; the study does not evaluate food-safety monitoring.
